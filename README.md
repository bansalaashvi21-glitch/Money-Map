# Money Map

### Live Tool: https://bansalaashvi21-glitch.github.io/Money-Map/

### Mapping your money. Understanding your financial picture.

**Money Map** is a Python-based financial analysis tool designed to help households understand their income, expenses, savings, and potential financial growth.

By turning basic financial information into meaningful calculations and insights, Money Map aims to make household financial analysis simpler, more structured, and accessible.

## Purpose

Managing money involves more than tracking how much is earned or spent. Understanding spending patterns, calculating savings, and exploring how money can grow over time are equally important.

Money Map was developed to help bridge this gap by using programming to organise financial data and present it in a way that is easier to understand.

## Key Features

### 1. Income and Expense Analysis
Analyses household income and expenditure to calculate the amount remaining after expenses and provide a clearer picture of household cash flow.

### 2. Savings Analysis
Calculates total savings and the savings rate, helping users understand how much of their income they retain.

### 3. Expense Analysis
Examines household spending patterns, categorises expenses, and helps identify major areas of expenditure and potential opportunities to improve spending habits.

### 4. Budget Analysis
Uses the 50/30/20 budgeting framework to help users understand how their spending is distributed across needs, wants, and savings.

### 5. Compound Growth Calculations
Demonstrates how savings or investments may grow over time through compounding, using user-defined assumptions such as the initial amount, rate of return, and time period.

### 6. Rule of 72
Uses the Rule of 72 to estimate how long an investment may take to double at a given annual rate of return.

### 7. Government Scheme Eligibility Assessment
Money Map checks whether a household qualifies for central government welfare schemes, including:

- Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY)
- Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY)
- Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
- Ayushman Bharat

Eligibility assessments are based on the financial information and other relevant criteria entered by the user.

### 8. Financial Summary
Brings together the different calculations into a consolidated financial overview, helping users interpret their household's financial position and identify potential areas for improvement.

## Example

Consider a household with the following monthly financial information:

| Financial Metric | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Monthly Income | ₹2,05,000 |
| Monthly Expenses | ₹1,57,841 |
| Monthly Surplus | ₹47,159 |
| Savings Rate | Approximately 23% |

Money Map uses this information to analyse the household's financial position and explore potential long-term growth through additional calculations.

## Technology

- **Python via Pyodide (WebAssembly):** Runs Python natively in the browser without requiring installation.
- **HTML:** User-facing interface.
- **GitHub Pages:** Hosting and deployment of the live tool.

## Disclaimer

Money Map is an educational financial analysis tool intended to help users understand financial information and explore basic financial concepts.

It does not provide personalised financial, investment, tax, or legal advice. Growth projections are illustrative and depend on the assumptions entered by the user. Government scheme eligibility assessments are indicative and should be verified against the official scheme guidelines.

## About the Project

Money Map began with the first level of my Family Ledger Project, which I conducted with my own family. During the process, I realised that there were areas where our finances could be improved, but these opportunities often went unnoticed unless the information was systematically organised. Even smaller or miscellaneous expenses were difficult to track, making it harder to identify where spending could be reduced.

I wanted families to gain a clear picture of their financial position and identify areas where they could potentially improve. Traditional budgeting might tell someone how much they have saved, but Money Map goes a step further by allowing them to visualise the impact of compounding and understand what even relatively small savings could potentially grow into over time.

One thing that surprised me was how a clearer understanding of a household's financial position could reveal opportunities beyond budgeting. Families might be eligible for certain government welfare schemes without even knowing it, simply because they had never systematically understood their financial standing.

Moving forward, I want Money Map to become a tool that helps families understand their finances clearly without making the process complicated or overwhelming. I hope to test it with 15 families, learn from their experiences, and use their feedback to make the tool more useful and accessible.
